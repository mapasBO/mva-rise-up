# Tarija - Phase 3: hexagon aggregation, choropleths and bivariate maps (guide section 5)

Script: `scripts/tarija/phase3_hotspots.py`. Log: `MVA/BOL/Tarija/phase3_log.csv`. QGIS project: `MVA/BOL/Tarija/MVA_Tarija.qgz`, group *Phase 3 - Hotspots (guide section 5)*.

**As in the guide**
- Hexagon grid with 500 m horizontal and vertical spacing (`Base_Layer/Tarija_Hexagon_500m.gpkg`).
- Zonal statistics (mean) of each dimension: `Combined/Tarija_Hexagon_500m_CC|URB|BIO.gpkg` (fields `CC_mean`, `URB_mean`, `BIO_mean`, plus cell counts).
- Choropleths with 3 equal-interval classes 0-0.33 / 0.33-0.66 / 0.66-max, white outline, guide colours (URB #e8e8e8/#eeaeae/#f47474; CC #e8e8e8/#aec3bc/#749f91).
- Bivariate map Urban over Climate with blending mode *Multiply*, and a 3x3 legend.

**Additions (documented deviations)**
- Grid extent taken from the urban area instead of the city boundary (in Tarija the boundary is the whole municipality); only the 550 hexagons that intersect the urban area are kept.
- Two more bivariate pairs with the same technique: Climate x Biodiversity and Urban x Biodiversity. Biodiversity colour ramp: #e8e8e8 / #c3b3d8 / #9972af.
- Hexagons of the combined vulnerability and a neighbourhood table with the four dimension means (`Combined/Tarija_Neighbourhoods_MVA.gpkg`).
- Legends generated by the script (`Combined/Tarija_Bivariate_Legend_*.png`) instead of the Bivariate Legend plugin.

**Results (550 hexagons)**

| Dimension | Low (0-0.33) | Medium (0.33-0.66) | High (0.66-max) | Mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Climate | 282 | 268 | 0 | 0.34 |
| Urban | 385 | 165 | 0 | 0.29 |
| Biodiversity | 16 | 239 | 294 | 0.64 |
| Combined | 10 | 540 | 0 | 0.42 |

Bivariate Urban x Climate (hexagons): low-low 220; climate medium only 165; urban medium only 62; **both medium 103** (no hexagon reaches the high class in either dimension). The 103 "both medium" hexagons are the Tarija hotspots under the guide classification; they lie on the northern, eastern and southern periphery.

**Reading note:** at 500 m the climate dimension shows the peripheral slopes (medium class), which were diluted at 12.5 m. With the guide's fixed classes, no hexagon reaches "high" in climate or urban vulnerability; the hotspots are therefore the medium-medium overlaps, not absolute high values.

**Complementary combined map (not in the guide):** `Tarija_Hexagon_500m_Combined` with quintile classes (style `Combined/Tarija_Hexagon_500m_Combined_quintiles.qml`; breaks 0.275 / 0.386 / 0.407 / 0.425 / 0.452 / 0.586). With the guide's 3 classes, 540 of 550 hexagons fall in the medium class, so quintiles are used to show relative differences within Tarija. Q5 (0.452-0.586) marks the 20% most vulnerable hexagons: northern, western and eastern edges and the south. These are relative hotspots, not absolute high vulnerability.

**Standard deviation classes for the combined maps (complementary, team decision 2026-11-25):** the guide's fixed classes put almost all the combined index in one or two classes. Classes are set at mean ± 0.5 and ± 1.5 standard deviations of each map: raster `Tarija_Combined_Vulnerability` (mean 0.415, SD 0.066): 0.315 / 0.382 / 0.448 / 0.515, cells 6 / 25 / 40 / 22 / 7 %; hexagons `Tarija_Hexagon_500m_Combined` (mean 0.420, SD 0.044): 0.353 / 0.398 / 0.442 / 0.487, hexagons 27 / 140 / 245 / 93 / 45. Styles: `Combined/Tarija_Combined_Vulnerability_StdDev.qml`, `Combined/Tarija_Hexagon_500m_Combined_StdDev.qml`; in the QGIS project both layers keep the guide style as a second named style. 'Very high' = more than 1.5 SD above the Tarija mean (relative, not absolute).

**Map layouts:** 24 A4 layouts in `MVA_Tarija.qgz`: 23 made by `scripts/tarija/phase3b_layouts.py` (indicators, vulnerability rasters, hexagon maps and the three bivariate maps with their 3x3 legend) and one made by hand (combined hexagons with a city-centre inset at 1:42,000). Neighbourhood boundaries are shown on all maps; 14 main neighbourhoods are labelled, chosen one per 2 km cell (largest neighbourhood in each cell) so names are spread evenly and do not overlap: Avaroa, B. Aeropuerto, Catedral, El Constructor, Los Chapacos, Lourdes, Morros Blancos, San Antonio, San Blas, San Jeronimo, San Luis, Tabladita II, Torrecillas, Virgen de Fatima. The neighbourhood layer covers 34% of the urban area (the consolidated centre), so the periphery has no neighbourhood names.

**Legends:** `scripts/tarija/phase3c_legends.py` loads the styles in `styles/tarija` and cleans every legend: a short two- or three-line EN/ES title instead of the file name, no "Band 1 (Gray)" entry, bilingual class labels with their value ranges (e.g. *Very low / Muy baja (0.00-0.20)*), and long categorical labels wrapped (BIO2, BIO3, URB3, combined raster, hexagon maps). Urban heat island (CC5) and biodiversity loss (BIO1) use the same 5 fixed classes as the other indicators. In URB3, class 0 (consolidated or no growth) is light grey (#ececec) so that new urban growth stands out. The script then exports all layouts to PNG at 300 dpi (`Data_Output/Maps_PNG`) and 150 dpi (`docs/img/tarija/maps`).
